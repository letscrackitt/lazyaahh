How to take a power nap that works

The Lazy Corner · Napping

A good short nap can leave you sharper for the rest of the afternoon. A bad one leaves you groggy and wide awake at midnight. The difference is mostly timing and setup.

Keep it short

Aim for 10 to 20 minutes. Longer naps can drop you into deep sleep, and waking from that is what causes the heavy, confused feeling. If you have time for a full 90 minutes, that is a complete sleep cycle and can also work, but it is a different habit.

Pick the right time

Early afternoon, roughly 1 to 3 pm, suits most people because energy naturally dips then. Napping late in the day can make it harder to fall asleep at night.

Set up your space

  • Find somewhere quiet and dim, or use an eye mask.
  • Lie down or recline if you can. A slightly cool room helps.
  • Set an alarm before you close your eyes, so you are not worrying about oversleeping.

Waking up well

Get up when the alarm rings instead of snoozing. Drink some water and step into daylight for a minute or two. Most grogginess fades quickly once you are moving.

If you cannot fall asleep

Resting with your eyes closed is still useful. Do not force it, and do not check the clock. Try the nap mode on our home page for a dark, quiet screen.

If you are very tired every day despite enough sleep at night, speak to a doctor rather than relying on naps.
